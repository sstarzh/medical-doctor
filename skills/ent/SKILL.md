# ENT (otolaryngology)

## Emergencies
- **Airway**: stridor, drooling, can't swallow saliva, muffled "hot potato" voice, trismus, tripod posture → epiglottitis, peritonsillar or retropharyngeal abscess, Ludwig angina. Emergency care; do not examine the throat in a child with drooling and stridor.
- **Sudden sensorineural hearing loss** (over hours to 3 days, one ear, no wax/infection): same-week audiogram and steroids; urgency because outcomes fall with delay.
- Posterior epistaxis or bleeding not controlled by 15–20 minutes of firm pressure, especially on anticoagulants.
- Vertigo with neurological signs (diplopia, dysarthria, ataxia, new headache, unable to walk): stroke until proven otherwise (HINTS by trained clinicians only).
- Facial nerve palsy sparing the forehead (central/stroke) vs whole face (Bell's palsy: prednisone within 72 h; check ears for zoster vesicles: Ramsay Hunt).

## Sore throat
Score with calc_centor_mcisaac. 0–1: no test, no antibiotics. 2–3: rapid strep test/culture, treat if positive. Penicillin V or amoxicillin first-line; check allergies. Unilateral severe pain, uvula deviation, trismus → peritonsillar abscess (drainage). Consider mononucleosis (avoid amoxicillin rash; no contact sports), acute HIV, gonorrhea.

## Ear
Acute otitis media in children: see pediatrics (watchful waiting criteria, amoxicillin high dose by weight via calc_weight_based_dose). Otitis externa: topical drops; malignant otitis externa in older diabetics (severe pain, granulation tissue) → urgent. Cholesteatoma: persistent foul discharge + hearing loss.

## Sinusitis
Most acute rhinosinusitis is viral. Bacterial if ≥10 days without improvement, severe onset (fever ≥39 °C + purulence ≥3 days), or "double worsening". Red flags: periorbital swelling, vision change, severe headache, confusion → orbital/intracranial spread, emergency.

## Vertigo
BPPV: brief positional episodes, positive Dix-Hallpike → Epley maneuver, no vestibular suppressants long-term. Vestibular neuritis: continuous days-long vertigo, no hearing loss. Ménière: episodes with hearing loss, tinnitus, aural fullness.

## Neck lump and voice
Adult neck mass persisting >2–3 weeks without infection, hoarseness >3 weeks (especially smokers), dysphagia, unilateral otalgia with a normal ear → ENT referral for head and neck cancer (HPV-related oropharyngeal cancer occurs in non-smokers).
