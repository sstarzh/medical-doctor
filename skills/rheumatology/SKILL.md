# Rheumatology

## Inflammatory or mechanical?
Inflammatory: morning stiffness >30–60 min, improves with use, swelling, warmth, systemic symptoms, raised CRP/ESR. Mechanical: worse with use, brief stiffness, no swelling. Count and map joints (small hand joints symmetric → RA pattern; DIP + nail changes → psoriatic; axial + young + better with exercise → spondyloarthritis).

## Must-not-miss
- **Acute hot swollen joint = septic arthritis until aspirated**, especially with fever, prosthetic joint, immunosuppression or injection drug use. Same-day joint aspiration (cell count, Gram stain, culture, crystals) before antibiotics if possible. Gout and infection can coexist.
- **Giant cell arteritis** (age ≥50): new headache, scalp tenderness, jaw claudication, visual symptoms, PMR symptoms, high ESR/CRP. Visual symptoms → emergency. Start high-dose glucocorticoids the same day if suspicion is high; do not wait for temporal artery biopsy/ultrasound.
- Systemic vasculitis: palpable purpura, hematuria/AKI, hemoptysis, mononeuritis multiplex → urgent.
- Cauda equina, malignancy and infection in back pain with red flags.

## Gout
Flare: NSAID, colchicine (low-dose regimen; reduce in CKD and with CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors) or glucocorticoid. Urate-lowering therapy (allopurinol, start low, titrate to urate <6 mg/dL) for recurrent flares, tophi, erosions, or CKD stage ≥3; flare prophylaxis for 3–6 months. HLA-B*58:01 testing before allopurinol in high-risk ancestry (Han Chinese, Korean with CKD, Thai, African American).

## Rheumatoid arthritis
Early referral (window of opportunity <3 months). Anti-CCP more specific than RF. Methotrexate first-line DMARD (folate, liver/blood monitoring, avoid in pregnancy and with heavy alcohol use; teratogen).

## Lupus and the ANA
A positive ANA is common in healthy people (especially low titer); test only with a compatible picture. Lupus features: photosensitive rash, oral ulcers, arthritis, serositis, cytopenias, nephritis (check urine and creatinine). Hydroxychloroquine for nearly all, with yearly eye screening after 5 years.

## Polymyalgia rheumatica
Age ≥50, bilateral shoulder/hip girdle pain and stiffness, high ESR/CRP, rapid response to prednisone 12.5–25 mg. Always screen for GCA symptoms.

## Steroid safety
Long-term glucocorticoids: bone protection (calcium, vitamin D, bisphosphonate when indicated), glucose and BP monitoring, PJP prophylaxis at high doses, never stop abruptly after >3 weeks.
