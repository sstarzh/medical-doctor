# Ophthalmology

## Emergencies (same-hour to same-day eye care)
- **Chemical injury**: irrigate immediately with water or saline for at least 15–30 minutes BEFORE travelling; alkali is worst.
- **Sudden painless vision loss**: central retinal artery occlusion (a stroke of the eye: emergency stroke pathway), retinal detachment, vitreous hemorrhage, giant cell arteritis (age ≥50 with headache/jaw claudication: start steroids, see rheumatology).
- **Acute angle-closure glaucoma**: painful red eye, blurred vision, halos around lights, headache, nausea/vomiting, mid-dilated poorly reactive pupil, hard eye. Can follow dilating drugs (anticholinergics, decongestants).
- **Flashes + new floaters or a curtain/shadow**: retinal detachment/tear: same-day dilated exam.
- Penetrating injury or suspected globe rupture (do not press on the eye; shield it), high-velocity foreign body (grinding/hammering metal).
- Contact-lens wearer with a painful red eye and light sensitivity: microbial keratitis (Pseudomonas, Acanthamoeba): same day; stop lenses.
- Herpes zoster on the tip of the nose (Hutchinson sign), orbital cellulitis (painful eye movements, proptosis, fever: emergency, especially in children).

## Red eye triage
Red flags: reduced vision, real pain (not grittiness), photophobia, abnormal pupil, corneal opacity, contact lenses, trauma, neonate. Any red flag → same-day eye assessment.
- Conjunctivitis (no red flags): viral (watery, recent cold, very contagious), bacterial (purulent, lids stuck; topical antibiotic), allergic (itch, bilateral). Neonatal conjunctivitis → urgent (gonococcal/chlamydial).
- Subconjunctival hemorrhage: painless, normal vision; check BP and anticoagulation if recurrent.
- Anterior uveitis: pain, photophobia, ciliary flush, small pupil; associated with spondyloarthritis, IBD, sarcoid.

## Medicines and the eye
Never prescribe topical steroid or anesthetic drops without an eye exam (herpes keratitis, ulcers). Hydroxychloroquine (retinal screening), ethambutol, amiodarone, topiramate (angle closure) and long-term steroids (cataract, glaucoma) have eye toxicity.

## Children
Leukocoria (white pupil reflex in photos) → urgent retinoblastoma referral. New squint/strabismus → refer (amblyopia risk). Vision screening per Bright Futures (preventive_care_due).
