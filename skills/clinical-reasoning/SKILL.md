# Clinical Reasoning (Attending Standard)

## 1. Gather and structure
- Minimum dataset: age, sex (and pregnancy possibility in anyone who could be pregnant), chief complaint with **onset, tempo, duration, severity, location, radiation, modifiers, associated symptoms**, PMH, PSH, medications (incl. OTC/supplements), allergies, social (tobacco, alcohol, drugs, occupation, travel, sexual history when relevant), family history, vitals, exam, labs, imaging.
- If key data are missing, state your assumption explicitly and list the questions that would most change management.

## 2. Problem representation
One sentence with **semantic qualifiers**: acute vs chronic, unilateral vs bilateral, constant vs episodic, inflammatory vs mechanical, host factors.
> "62-year-old man with diabetes and smoking history with 2 hours of acute, pressure-like, exertional substernal chest pain with diaphoresis."

## 3. Differential diagnosis
- Generate by **illness scripts** (epidemiology → time course → syndrome → pathophysiology) and cross-check with an anatomic/systems sweep (VINDICATE: vascular, infectious, neoplastic, drugs/toxins, inflammatory/autoimmune, congenital, iatrogenic/idiopathic, trauma, endocrine/metabolic).
- Rank by **probability**, but always list **can't-miss** diagnoses (high harm if missed) separately, even when unlikely. Examples by complaint:
  - Chest pain: ACS, aortic dissection, PE, tension pneumothorax, tamponade, esophageal rupture.
  - Headache: SAH, meningitis, cerebral venous thrombosis, giant cell arteritis (>50), carbon monoxide, mass/ICP, preeclampsia (pregnant/postpartum).
  - Abdominal pain: AAA, mesenteric ischemia, ectopic pregnancy, perforation, obstruction, appendicitis, testicular/ovarian torsion, MI (epigastric), DKA.
  - Dyspnea: PE, ACS/heart failure, pneumothorax, anaphylaxis, sepsis, metabolic acidosis.
  - Back pain: AAA, cauda equina, spinal epidural abscess, malignancy, dissection.
  - Syncope: arrhythmia, structural heart disease (AS, HCM), PE, hemorrhage, dissection.
  - Fever: sepsis, meningitis, neutropenic fever, endocarditis, necrotizing soft-tissue infection, malaria (travel).

## 4. Bayesian reasoning
- Estimate pretest probability (prevalence in this setting + validated scores — **always compute scores with the calculator tools**).
- Post-test odds = pretest odds × likelihood ratio. LR+ >10 or LR− <0.1 changes management substantially; LR 0.5–2 barely moves it.
- Know the **test and treatment thresholds**: below test threshold → stop testing; above treatment threshold → treat.
- Sensitive tests rule out (SnNout); specific tests rule in (SpPin).

## 5. Debias before finalizing
- **Anchoring / premature closure**: "What else could this be? What doesn't fit?"
- **Availability, framing, diagnosis momentum**: re-derive from raw data, not prior labels.
- **Search satisficing**: after finding one abnormality, look for a second (e.g., fracture + syncope cause).
- **Psych-out error**: never attribute symptoms to anxiety/psychiatric illness until organic causes are reasonably excluded.
- **Base rates**: common things are common, but red flags override.
- Consider medication adverse effects for every new symptom.

## 6. Output
Ranked differential table (diagnosis | likelihood | for | against), can't-miss marked ⚠️, then the **single most discriminating next steps**. State what result would change the plan.
