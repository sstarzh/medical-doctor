# Psychiatry

## Suicide risk — always address directly
- Ask: thoughts of death or suicide? Plan? Intent? Access to means (firearms, medication stockpiles)? Prior attempts? Protective factors?
- Columbia (C-SSRS) screener: yes to active ideation with method/intent/plan, or recent behavior → emergency evaluation.
- Imminent risk → emergency services, do not leave alone, remove lethal means. Provide crisis lines: US 988 (call/text/chat), UK & IE Samaritans 116 123, elsewhere findahelpline.com.
- Lower risk → collaborative safety plan (warning signs, coping strategies, contacts, professionals, means restriction) and close follow-up.
- Respond with warmth and without judgment; validate distress.

## Depression and anxiety
- Screen PHQ-2 → PHQ-9 (5/10/15/20 = mild/moderate/moderately severe/severe; item 9 positive → suicide assessment). GAD-7 (5/10/15).
- Exclude medical causes (thyroid, anemia, B12, substance, medication) and **screen for bipolar disorder** (prior mania/hypomania, family history) before starting an antidepressant.
- First-line: psychotherapy (CBT) and/or SSRI (sertraline, escitalopram) or SNRI. Reassess at 4–6 weeks; continue 6–12 months after remission. Black-box: suicidality monitoring <25 years.
- Serotonin syndrome and QT (citalopram >40 mg/day, >20 mg if >60 y) — check interactions.

## Psychosis / mania
New psychosis needs medical workup (delirium, substances, steroids, autoimmune/infectious encephalitis, tumors). Acute agitation: verbal de-escalation first. Lithium: narrow therapeutic index, check renal/thyroid, interactions (NSAIDs, ACEi, thiazides raise levels). Clozapine: ANC monitoring, myocarditis, constipation.

## Substance use
- Alcohol withdrawal: CIWA-Ar guided symptom-triggered benzodiazepines; thiamine before glucose; seizures 6–48 h, delirium tremens 48–96 h (emergency). Relapse prevention: naltrexone, acamprosate (renal), disulfiram.
- Opioid use disorder: buprenorphine or methadone reduce mortality (no X-waiver needed since 2023); naloxone for every patient at risk. Precipitated withdrawal risk with buprenorphine after fentanyl — low-dose or high-dose initiation strategies.
- Stimulants: no FDA-approved pharmacotherapy; contingency management is evidence-based.

## Eating disorders
Medical instability (HR <50, orthostasis, hypokalemia, hypophosphatemia, BMI <15 or rapid loss) → hospitalization; refeeding syndrome risk.
