# Pediatrics

## Ground rules
- Children are not small adults: always use **age and weight**. Verify every weight-based dose with `drug_label` pediatric sections and never exceed the adult maximum.
- Never recommend: aspirin (<18 y, Reye syndrome) for viral illness; honey <12 months (botulism); OTC cough/cold medicines <4 years (FDA/AAP); codeine or tramadol <12 years (FDA contraindication).

## Normal vital signs (approximate, awake)
| Age | HR | RR | SBP |
|---|---|---|---|
| Newborn–3 mo | 100–180 | 30–60 | 60–90 |
| 3–12 mo | 100–160 | 25–45 | 70–100 |
| 1–3 y | 90–150 | 20–35 | 80–110 |
| 4–5 y | 80–140 | 20–30 | 80–110 |
| 6–12 y | 70–120 | 16–25 | 90–120 |
| 13+ y | 60–100 | 12–20 | 100–130 |
Hypotension threshold (1–10 y): SBP < 70 + (2 × age in years).

## Fever
- <90 days with ≥38.0 °C: emergency evaluation (AAP 2021 febrile infant guideline; 8–21 days: full workup incl. LP and empiric antibiotics).
- Older children: assess appearance (Pediatric Assessment Triangle: appearance, work of breathing, circulation to skin), hydration, focus. Fever height alone does not predict serious infection; fever ≥5 days → consider Kawasaki disease.
- Antipyretics for comfort: acetaminophen (from birth by weight) or ibuprofen (≥6 months).

## Respiratory
- Bronchiolitis (<2 y): supportive (suction, hydration, O2 if SpO2 <90%); no routine bronchodilators, steroids, or antibiotics. Nirsevimab/maternal RSV vaccine for prevention.
- Croup: single-dose dexamethasone 0.6 mg/kg (max 16 mg — confirm locally; 0.15 mg/kg effective for mild); nebulized epinephrine for stridor at rest.
- Acute otitis media: watchful waiting is acceptable for many ≥6 months; amoxicillin high-dose when treating.

## Dehydration
Mild-moderate → oral rehydration solution (small frequent volumes; ondansetron single dose can help ≥6 months). Severe (lethargy, poor perfusion, no urine) → emergency, IV 20 mL/kg isotonic bolus.

## Development and prevention
Screen milestones at well visits; autism screening (M-CHAT) at 18 and 24 months. Follow the CDC/AAP immunization schedule. Safe sleep (back to sleep, no bed-sharing) for infants.

## Safeguarding
Injuries inconsistent with developmental stage or history, bruises in non-mobile infants (TEN-4-FACESp), delayed presentation → child-protection evaluation; clinicians are mandated reporters.
