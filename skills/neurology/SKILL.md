# Neurology

## Stroke / TIA
- BE-FAST; record **last known well**. Check glucose (hypoglycemia mimics stroke).
- IV thrombolysis (alteplase/tenecteplase) within 4.5 h if eligible; thrombectomy for large-vessel occlusion up to 24 h with imaging selection.
- TIA is an emergency: same-day evaluation (ABCD² underestimates risk; image vessels). Minor stroke/high-risk TIA → DAPT (aspirin + clopidogrel) for 21 days (POINT/CHANCE) unless contraindicated.
- Secondary prevention: high-intensity statin, BP <130/80, AF → anticoagulation, carotid stenosis 70–99% symptomatic → revascularization within 2 weeks.

## Headache red flags (SNOOP4)
Systemic symptoms (fever, weight loss, cancer, immunosuppression), Neurologic deficits/confusion, Onset sudden (thunderclap), Older (new headache >50 → GCA: ESR/CRP, temporal symptoms, jaw claudication, vision change → steroids now), Positional, Precipitated by Valsalva/exertion, Papilledema, Progressive/pattern change, Pregnancy/postpartum.
Primary headache: migraine acute therapy (NSAIDs, triptans, gepants), prevention if ≥4 days/month; avoid medication overuse (simple analgesics ≥15 days/month, triptans/opioids ≥10).

## Seizure
- First unprovoked seizure: glucose, electrolytes, toxicology, pregnancy test, EEG, MRI. Driving restrictions per local law.
- Status epilepticus (≥5 min or recurrent without recovery): benzodiazepine (IM midazolam 10 mg / IV lorazepam 4 mg), then levetiracetam, fosphenytoin or valproate (avoid valproate in pregnancy potential).

## Dizziness
Classify by timing/triggers (acute continuous, episodic triggered, episodic spontaneous). Acute vestibular syndrome: HINTS exam (in trained hands) more sensitive than early MRI for posterior stroke; any central sign → emergency imaging. BPPV: Dix-Hallpike + Epley maneuver.

## Weakness localization
UMN (spasticity, hyperreflexia, Babinski) vs LMN (atrophy, fasciculations, hyporeflexia) vs NMJ (fatigable, ocular/bulbar — myasthenia) vs muscle (proximal, CK). Ascending weakness + areflexia → Guillain-Barré (check vital capacity/NIF; ICU if declining). Acute cord compression → emergency MRI.

## Altered mental status / delirium
Acute onset, fluctuating, inattention (CAM). Search for causes: infection, drugs (anticholinergics, sedatives, opioids), metabolic (glucose, Na, Ca, uremia, hepatic), hypoxia/hypercapnia, stroke, seizure, withdrawal, urinary retention/constipation. Use GCS (`calc_glasgow_coma_scale`) for level of consciousness.

## Cognitive decline
Rule out reversible contributors (B12, TSH, depression, medications, OSA, NPH triad). MoCA for screening. Anti-amyloid therapies require specialist assessment (ARIA risk, APOE genotyping).
