# Hematology and oncology

## Emergencies
- **Neutropenic fever**: temperature ≥38.0 °C in someone on chemotherapy (or ANC <500) → emergency department now for cultures and IV antibiotics within 60 minutes. Never "wait and see".
- **Spinal cord compression**: known cancer + new back pain, leg weakness, numbness, bladder/bowel change → same-day MRI and steroids.
- Hypercalcemia of malignancy (confusion, thirst, constipation), tumor lysis (after starting treatment: K+, phosphate, uric acid, creatinine), SVC syndrome (face/arm swelling, breathlessness), hyperviscosity.
- **Bleeding**: on anticoagulation with head injury, black/bloody stool, or large bleed → emergency; platelets <20k with bleeding.
- Thrombosis red flags: unilateral leg swelling (DVT), pleuritic pain + breathlessness (PE: see pulmonology).

## Anemia
Classify by MCV. Microcytic: iron deficiency (ferritin <30 ng/mL; in adults find the source: GI evaluation for men and postmenopausal women; celiac testing), thalassemia trait. Macrocytic: B12/folate, alcohol, liver, hypothyroidism, drugs (methotrexate, hydroxyurea), MDS. Normocytic: CKD, inflammation, hemolysis (LDH, haptoglobin, bilirubin, reticulocytes), bleeding. Pancytopenia or blasts → urgent hematology.

## Anticoagulation
Choose and dose DOACs by indication, kidney function (calc_creatinine_clearance_cockcroft_gault), weight, age and interacting drugs (check with drug_interaction_check). Bleeding risk: calc_has_bled; stroke risk in AF: calc_cha2ds2_vasc. Warfarin interacts with many antibiotics and antifungals (INR rise).

## Cancer warning signs (refer urgently)
Unexplained weight loss (>5% in 6–12 months), iron-deficiency anemia in older adults, rectal bleeding with change in bowel habit (≥40–50), postmenopausal bleeding, breast lump, persistent hoarseness >3 weeks, dysphagia, hemoptysis, painless jaundice, new lymph node >2 cm or growing >4–6 weeks, night sweats with fevers.

## Screening (USPSTF; check preventive_care_due)
Colorectal 45–75, breast mammography 40–74 biennial, cervical 21–65, lung LDCT 50–80 with ≥20 pack-years, prostate PSA 55–69 shared decision. Earlier/more intensive with family history or genetic syndromes (BRCA, Lynch): offer genetic counseling.

## Living with cancer
Ask about the treatment regimen and cycle day (nadir 7–14 days), central lines, and the oncology team's contact; drug interactions with oral chemotherapy are common; vaccines (no live vaccines when immunosuppressed).
