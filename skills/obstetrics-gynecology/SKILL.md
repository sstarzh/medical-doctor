# Obstetrics & Gynecology

## Pregnancy test first
Any person who could be pregnant with abdominal pain, bleeding, syncope, or before imaging/teratogenic drugs → pregnancy test. Ectopic pregnancy must be excluded in pain/bleeding with positive hCG and no confirmed intrauterine pregnancy.

## Emergencies in pregnancy and postpartum
- Vaginal bleeding with pain or instability (ectopic, abruption, previa — no digital exam if previa possible).
- Hypertension ≥140/90 after 20 weeks → preeclampsia workup; ≥160/110 is severe → treat within 30–60 min (IV labetalol, IV hydralazine, or immediate-release oral nifedipine); severe features (headache, visual change, RUQ pain, platelets <100k, creatinine >1.1, pulmonary edema) → magnesium sulfate and delivery planning. Preeclampsia can occur up to 6 weeks postpartum.
- Decreased fetal movement, rupture of membranes, preterm contractions → obstetric assessment.
- Postpartum: hemorrhage, PE/DVT (risk elevated ~12 weeks), cardiomyopathy (dyspnea, edema), postpartum depression/psychosis (psychosis is an emergency).

## Medications in pregnancy/lactation
Use FDA label sections 8.1/8.2 via `drug_label`. Generally preferred: acetaminophen (lowest effective dose), labetalol/nifedipine/methyldopa (HTN), insulin (diabetes; metformin acceptable in some), LMWH (anticoagulation), most penicillins/cephalosporins. Avoid: ACEi/ARB, warfarin (except some mechanical valves), statins, isotretinoin, valproate, methotrexate, mycophenolate, NSAIDs ≥20 weeks (oligohydramnios; avoid entirely ≥30 weeks).
Low-dose aspirin (81 mg) from 12–28 weeks for preeclampsia risk (USPSTF). Folic acid 0.4–0.8 mg daily periconception (4 mg if prior NTD).

## Contraception
Use CDC US Medical Eligibility Criteria (2024). Estrogen-containing methods are category 4 with migraine with aura, age ≥35 and smoking ≥15 cigarettes/day, history of VTE, <21 days postpartum, uncontrolled HTN. LARC (IUD, implant) most effective. Emergency contraception: copper IUD most effective; ulipristal up to 120 h; levonorgestrel up to 72 h (less effective with BMI >30).

## Gynecology
- Abnormal uterine bleeding: PALM-COEIN; endometrial sampling if ≥45 y or younger with unopposed estrogen risk.
- Postmenopausal bleeding → transvaginal ultrasound (endometrium >4 mm) or biopsy.
- Cervical cancer screening (USPSTF 2024 draft/2018): 21–29 cytology q3y; 30–65 primary HPV q5y, co-testing q5y, or cytology q3y.
- Pelvic inflammatory disease: low threshold to treat (ceftriaxone + doxycycline + metronidazole).
