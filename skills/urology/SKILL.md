# Urology

## Emergencies
- **Testicular torsion**: sudden severe testicular pain ± vomiting, high-riding testis, absent cremasteric reflex, any age (peak adolescents). Emergency surgery within 6 hours saves the testis; do not delay for ultrasound if suspicion is high.
- **Acute urinary retention**: unable to pass urine, painful distended bladder → catheter today. Look for causes: BPH, anticholinergics/antihistamines, opioids, constipation, cauda equina (back pain, saddle numbness, leg weakness: emergency MRI).
- Fournier gangrene (perineal pain, swelling, fever, diabetes), priapism >4 h, paraphimosis, obstructed infected kidney (stone + fever), urosepsis.

## Hematuria
Visible hematuria in adults is cancer until proven otherwise (bladder, kidney): CT urogram and cystoscopy referral, especially ≥35, smokers, occupational exposures. Microscopic hematuria: confirm on microscopy (≥3 RBC/hpf), exclude infection and menstruation, then risk-stratify (AUA). Hematuria with protein, casts or high BP → think glomerular (see nephrology).

## Urinary tract infection
Uncomplicated cystitis in non-pregnant women: nitrofurantoin (avoid if CrCl <30), TMP-SMX (check sulfa allergy and local resistance), or fosfomycin. Men, pregnancy, fever/flank pain (pyelonephritis), catheter, or recurrence → complicated: urine culture first. Pyelonephritis with vomiting, sepsis or obstruction → emergency. Asymptomatic bacteriuria: do not treat except in pregnancy and before urologic procedures.

## Prostate
- BPH: IPSS score; alpha-blocker (tamsulosin) for symptoms, add 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor for large prostates; avoid decongestants and anticholinergics.
- PSA: shared decision 55–69 (USPSTF C); discuss false positives and overdiagnosis. Raised PSA: repeat, exclude infection/instrumentation, then urology (MRI-first pathways).

## Erectile dysfunction
A cardiovascular warning sign: check BP, lipids, glucose. PDE5 inhibitors are **contraindicated with nitrates** (and riociguat); caution with alpha-blockers.

## Incontinence
Stress (pelvic floor training), urge (bladder training, then mirabegron preferred over anticholinergics in older adults: Beers), overflow (retention). Exclude UTI and new neurology.

## Children
Undescended testis (refer by 6 months), phimosis is physiological in young boys, febrile UTI in infants → workup (see pediatric-infectious-disease), testicular torsion in teens.
