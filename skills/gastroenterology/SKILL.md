# Gastroenterology & Hepatology

## Abdominal pain
- Localize by quadrant but always consider extra-abdominal causes (MI, DKA, pneumonia, testicular torsion, ectopic pregnancy) and vascular catastrophes (AAA, mesenteric ischemia — pain out of proportion, AF/vascular disease, elevated lactate late).
- Pregnancy test in anyone who could be pregnant. Older adults and immunosuppressed may have minimal signs.

## Upper GI bleeding
- Resuscitate: two large-bore IVs, transfuse at Hb <7 g/dL (<8 with cardiovascular disease), IV PPI; cirrhosis → ceftriaxone + octreotide.
- Glasgow-Blatchford score 0–1 → outpatient management possible. Endoscopy within 24 h.
- Hold/reverse anticoagulants per severity; restart in most after hemostasis.

## Liver
- Interpret pattern (see lab-interpretation). Workup: viral hepatitis serologies, ferritin/transferrin saturation, autoimmune markers, ceruloplasmin (<40 y), alcohol history, metabolic dysfunction (MASLD).
- MASLD risk stratification: FIB-4 (<1.3 low risk; >2.67 high → elastography/hepatology).
- Cirrhosis: prognosis with `calc_meld_na`; screen HCC with ultrasound ± AFP every 6 months; varices screening; avoid NSAIDs; acetaminophen ≤2 g/day is generally acceptable.
- Decompensation (ascites, variceal bleed, encephalopathy, jaundice) → hepatology referral and transplant evaluation if MELD ≥15.
- Acetaminophen overdose: Rumack-Matthew nomogram from 4 h level for single acute ingestion; N-acetylcysteine early, do not wait if presentation >8 h.

## Pancreatitis
2 of 3: typical pain, lipase >3× ULN, imaging. Early goal-directed moderate fluid resuscitation (lactated Ringer's; avoid aggressive fluids — WATERFALL), early oral feeding, find cause (gallstones → cholecystectomy same admission; triglycerides; alcohol; drugs).

## Lower GI
- IBS (Rome IV) only without alarm features: age >45 new onset, weight loss, bleeding, anemia, nocturnal symptoms, family history of CRC/IBD → colonoscopy. Fecal calprotectin helps separate IBD from IBS.
- C. difficile: fidaxomicin preferred (or oral vancomycin).

## Screening
Colorectal cancer screening age 45–75 (USPSTF 2021): colonoscopy q10y, FIT annually, stool DNA-FIT q1–3y, or CT colonography q5y. Earlier for family history.
