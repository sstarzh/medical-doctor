# Nephrology

## Always
- Compute kidney function with the tools: calc_egfr_ckd_epi_2021 (staging), calc_creatinine_clearance_cockcroft_gault (drug dosing), calc_bedside_schwartz_egfr in children. Compare with prior values in the chart: the trend decides AKI vs CKD.
- Review every medicine for renal dosing and nephrotoxins (NSAIDs, aminoglycosides, contrast, high-dose PPIs, lithium) and the "triple whammy" (NSAID + ACE inhibitor/ARB + diuretic).

## Acute kidney injury (KDIGO)
Creatinine rise ≥0.3 mg/dL in 48 h, or ≥1.5× baseline in 7 days, or urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h for 6 h.
- Prerenal (volume loss, heart failure, cirrhosis, NSAIDs/ACEi), intrinsic (ATN, interstitial nephritis from drugs, glomerulonephritis: blood + protein in urine, high BP), postrenal (retention, stones, prostate: bladder scan/ultrasound).
- Same-day/emergency care if: K+ ≥6.0 or ECG changes, fluid overload with breathlessness, uremic symptoms (confusion, pericarditis), anuria, or rapidly rising creatinine with active urine sediment.

## Chronic kidney disease
- Stage by eGFR (G1–G5) AND albuminuria (UACR A1 <30, A2 30–300, A3 >300 mg/g); both predict risk.
- Treat: BP target per guideline (generally <120–130 systolic if tolerated), ACEi or ARB for albuminuria, **SGLT2 inhibitor** for eGFR ≥20 (with or without diabetes), finerenone for diabetic CKD with albuminuria, statin, avoid nephrotoxins.
- Refer to nephrology: eGFR <30, UACR >300 mg/g, rapid decline (>5 mL/min/1.73 m² per year), hematuria with proteinuria, resistant hypertension, suspected genetic/glomerular disease.

## Electrolytes
- **Hyperkalemia**: K+ ≥6.0 or any ECG change (peaked T, wide QRS) → emergency (calcium, insulin+glucose, removal). Rule out hemolyzed sample. Review ACEi/ARB, MRA, trimethoprim, potassium supplements.
- **Hyponatremia**: Na+ <125 or symptomatic (confusion, seizures, vomiting) → emergency. Assess volume status, glucose (calc_corrected_sodium_hyperglycemia), osmolality, urine sodium; thiazides and SSRIs are common causes. Correct slowly (≤8–10 mmol/L per 24 h) to avoid osmotic demyelination.
- Hypercalcemia: use calc_corrected_calcium; >14 mg/dL or symptomatic → urgent.

## Kidney stones
Renal colic: NSAID first-line if kidney function allows; tamsulosin aids passage of distal stones 5–10 mm. Emergency: fever/infection with obstruction (needs drainage), single kidney, AKI, intractable pain or vomiting. Prevention: fluids to >2.5 L urine/day, metabolic workup after recurrence.
