# Well-Child Care & Development

## Growth
- Plot on **WHO charts from 0–2 years** and **CDC charts from 2–19 years**; correct for prematurity until 2 years.
- Concerning: weight crossing ≥2 major percentile lines downward, weight-for-length <5th percentile, or head circumference crossing lines (micro-/macrocephaly).
- **BMI-for-age** (2+ years): overweight 85th–<95th percentile; obesity ≥95th; severe obesity ≥120% of the 95th percentile. AAP 2023 CPG: offer intensive health behavior and lifestyle treatment; pharmacotherapy may be offered from age 12 (e.g., GLP-1 RA) and metabolic/bariatric surgery evaluation from 13 for severe obesity. Use person-first, non-stigmatizing language.
- Short stature: compare to mid-parental height and growth velocity; slowing velocity is more concerning than a low but steady percentile.

## Developmental milestones (CDC/AAP 2022: what most children — 75% — do by this age)
| Age | Milestones |
|---|---|
| 2 mo | Calms when spoken to, smiles at people, holds head up during tummy time |
| 4 mo | Chuckles, brings hands to mouth, holds head steady unsupported |
| 6 mo | Laughs, rolls front to back, takes turns making sounds |
| 9 mo | Sits without support, responds to name, babbles "mamama/bababa", stranger awareness |
| 12 mo | Waves bye-bye, pulls to stand, walks holding furniture, pincer grasp |
| 15 mo | Tries 1–2 words besides "mama/dada", takes a few steps alone, points to ask |
| 18 mo | Tries ≥3 words, walks alone, points to show interest, follows 1-step directions |
| 24 mo | Says ≥2 words together, points to things in a book, kicks a ball, notices when others are upset |
| 30 mo | Says ~50 words, two-word phrases with action words, uses things to pretend |
| 3 y | Conversation with back-and-forth, says name, draws a circle when shown |
| 4 y | Sentences of ≥4 words, tells a story, hops on one foot |
| 5 y | Tells a story with ≥2 events, counts to 10, buttons some buttons |

**Red flags at any age**: loss of previously acquired skills (regression), no babbling by 12 months, no single words by 16 months, no two-word phrases by 24 months, persistent fisting/asymmetry, not walking by 18 months, parental concern. Refer to early intervention without waiting for a diagnosis (US: Part C for <3 years; school district ≥3 years); check hearing.

## Screening (Bright Futures/AAP periodicity schedule)
- General developmental screening (e.g., ASQ) at 9, 18, and 30 months; **autism-specific (M-CHAT-R/F) at 18 and 24 months**.
- Maternal depression at 1, 2, 4, 6 months; lead risk/testing at 12 and 24 months (required for Medicaid); hemoglobin around 12 months; vision and hearing per schedule; fluoride varnish from first tooth; universal lipid screening once at 9–11 and once at 17–21 years.
- Adolescents: annual depression screening (PHQ-A) from 12, suicide risk screening, substance use (CRAFFT), HIV test at least once at 15–18, STI screening if sexually active.

## Immunizations
- Follow the AAP immunization schedule and the CDC/ACIP schedule. Where they diverge (as they have since 2025), state both and the reason, and follow AAP for pediatric recommendations unless local law/policy requires otherwise.
- Use catch-up schedules for delayed vaccination; minimum intervals matter.
- Vaccine hesitancy: listen, ask what worries them, use a presumptive recommendation, share specific evidence (no link between MMR and autism), and keep the relationship — never dismiss the family.

## Nutrition, sleep, screens, safety
- Exclusive breastfeeding about 6 months, continuing with complementary foods ≥1–2 years as desired; iron-fortified formula if not breastfed. Introduce allergens (peanut) early, around 4–6 months, after risk assessment for severe eczema/egg allergy (NIAID 2017). Whole cow's milk from 12 months (≤16–24 oz/day — excess causes iron deficiency). No juice under 1 year.
- Sleep needs: infants 12–16 h (incl. naps), 1–2 y 11–14 h, 3–5 y 10–13 h, 6–12 y 9–12 h, teens 8–10 h.
- Screens: avoid except video chat under 18–24 months; co-view and keep a family media plan; no screens in bedrooms at night.
- Injury prevention: rear-facing car seat as long as the seat allows (at least to age 2), then forward-facing harness, booster until the belt fits (~4'9"/145 cm); **safe firearm storage** (locked, unloaded, ammunition separate); water safety and swim lessons; safe medication storage; window guards; helmets.

## Behavior and learning
- **ADHD (AAP 2019)**: ages 4–18; criteria in ≥2 settings with teacher input. Preschool: parent training in behavior management first. School-age: FDA-approved medication (stimulant first) plus behavioral/school supports. Screen for comorbid anxiety, learning disorders, sleep problems.
- **Autism**: early signs include limited eye contact, no pointing to share, no response to name by 12 months, repetitive behaviors, regression; refer for diagnostic evaluation and early intervention concurrently.
- Toilet training typically 2–4 years; enuresis is normal until ~5 years (alarm or desmopressin after that).

## Adolescent health
- See the adolescent alone for part of every visit; explain confidentiality and its limits (risk of serious harm).
- **HEADSSS**: Home, Education/employment, Activities, Drugs, Sexuality, Suicide/depression, Safety (+ eating, social media).
- Sexual health: HPV vaccine (start 9–12), contraception counseling with LARC as first-line options, condoms for STI prevention, PrEP where appropriate. Consent laws for confidential care vary by state/country.
- Eating disorders: screen; bradycardia, orthostasis, or electrolyte problems → hospitalization criteria (see psychiatry).
- Gender-diverse youth: use their chosen name and pronouns, assess mental health and safety; refer to multidisciplinary specialty care for gender-affirming care questions, noting that the law varies by jurisdiction.
- Sports pre-participation exam: screen for exertional syncope/chest pain and family history of sudden cardiac death.
