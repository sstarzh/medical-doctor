# Geriatrics

## Think differently
- Atypical presentations: infection without fever, MI without chest pain, abdominal catastrophes with soft abdomen, delirium as the only sign of serious illness.
- Every new symptom: ask "could this be a drug?" (prescribing cascade).
- Function (ADLs/IADLs), cognition, mood, mobility, continence, social support, and goals of care belong in the assessment.

## Delirium
Acute, fluctuating inattention — medical emergency with high mortality. Search for precipitants (infection, drugs, retention, constipation, pain, hypoxia, metabolic, sleep loss). Non-pharmacologic first (reorientation, glasses/hearing aids, sleep hygiene, mobilization). Avoid benzodiazepines (except alcohol withdrawal) and anticholinergics; antipsychotics only for severe distress/danger, lowest dose, shortest time.

## Falls
Ask annually. After a fall: orthostatic vitals, medication review (sedatives, antihypertensives, hypoglycemics), vision, gait/strength (Timed Up and Go >12 s), feet/footwear, home safety, vitamin D if deficient, osteoporosis evaluation. Exercise (strength and balance) reduces falls.

## Polypharmacy
- AGS Beers Criteria 2023 and STOPP/START. Run `drug_interaction_check` on the whole list.
- Renal dosing via Cockcroft-Gault (`calc_creatinine_clearance_cockcroft_gault`) — eGFR overestimates clearance in low muscle mass.
- Deprescribe when harm or burden exceeds benefit, or time-to-benefit exceeds life expectancy (e.g., tight A1c, statins for primary prevention in advanced frailty).

## Glycemic and BP targets
Healthy older adult A1c <7.0–7.5%; complex/intermediate <8.0%; very complex/poor health — avoid hypoglycemia and symptomatic hyperglycemia rather than target A1c. Avoid orthostatic hypotension.

## Screening and goals
Cancer screening generally stops at 75–85 depending on life expectancy and prior results (CRC USPSTF: individualize 76–85). Discuss advance directives, health-care proxy, and code status; align care with what matters most.
