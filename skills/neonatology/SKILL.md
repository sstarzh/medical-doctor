# Neonatology & Young Infants

## Normal newborn course
- Weight loss up to ~7% is common in breastfed newborns; **>10% needs evaluation** (feeding, dehydration, hypernatremia, jaundice). Regain birth weight by 10–14 days, then ~20–30 g/day for the first 3 months.
- Feeds 8–12 times per 24 h; by day 5, ≥6 wet diapers and ≥3–4 yellow stools daily for breastfed babies.
- First meconium within 24–48 h (delay → Hirschsprung, cystic fibrosis/meconium ileus).
- Normal: sneezing, hiccups, periodic breathing (pauses <20 s), erythema toxicum, milia, Mongolian spots (document), mild breast swelling, vaginal bleeding from maternal hormones.

## Jaundice (AAP 2022 hyperbilirubinemia guideline)
- Jaundice in the **first 24 h is pathologic** (hemolysis) → same-day bilirubin.
- Visual estimation is unreliable: measure transcutaneous or serum bilirubin. Phototherapy and escalation thresholds depend on **gestational age, hours of age, and neurotoxicity risk factors** (albumin <3.0, isoimmune hemolysis, G6PD deficiency, sepsis, clinical instability) — use the AAP 2022 curves/BiliTool, not memory.
- Acute bilirubin encephalopathy (lethargy, poor feeding, hypotonia then hypertonia/arching, high-pitched cry) → emergency (escalation of care/exchange transfusion).
- Jaundice beyond 2 weeks (3 weeks if breastfed) or **pale/acholic stools or dark urine** → fractionated bilirubin; conjugated hyperbilirubinemia = biliary atresia until excluded (outcome depends on surgery before ~60 days).

## Fever and infection
- Any temperature ≥38.0 °C (rectal) under 3 months → same-day medical evaluation; under 28 days → hospital, full sepsis workup with LP, empiric antibiotics. Use `calc_pecarn_febrile_infant` for well-appearing infants ≤60 days with labs (AAP 2021 age bands).
- **Neonatal HSV** (usually days 5–21): vesicles, seizures, hypothermia, ill appearance, elevated transaminases, CSF pleocytosis, or maternal genital lesions → HSV PCR studies and IV acyclovir — maternal history is often negative.
- Hypothermia (<36.0 °C) and poor feeding can be the only signs of sepsis.
- Early-onset sepsis risk in infants ≥34 weeks: Kaiser EOS calculator using maternal risk factors.

## Breathing, color, events
- Cyanosis, persistent tachypnea >60, grunting, or apnea >20 s → emergency. Consider critical congenital heart disease (CCHD screening may miss lesions; check femoral pulses, murmur, hepatomegaly).
- **BRUE** (brief resolved unexplained event, AAP 2016): <1 year, <1 min, resolved, unexplained, with color change, breathing irregularity, tone change, or decreased responsiveness. **Lower-risk**: >60 days, gestational age ≥32 weeks and post-conceptional age ≥45 weeks, first event, <1 minute, no CPR by trained provider, normal history and exam → education, brief monitoring, follow-up. Otherwise evaluate (abuse, infection, seizure, cardiac, metabolic, airway).

## Feeding problems
- Spit-up ("happy spitter") is normal and peaks ~4 months; **GERD disease** requires poor weight gain or significant distress. Do not use acid suppression for uncomplicated infant reflux (NASPGHAN/ESPGHAN 2018); first try feeding modifications or a 2–4 week trial of eliminating cow's-milk protein.
- Red flags: bilious or projectile vomiting, blood, poor weight gain, lethargy, bulging fontanelle, onset after 6 months.
- Colic: crying >3 h/day, >3 days/week in a well, thriving infant; peaks ~6 weeks. Exclude illness (fever, hair tourniquet, corneal abrasion, fracture, hernia, UTI). Support caregivers and warn about shaking ("Period of PURPLE Crying").
- Hypoglycemia risk in infants of diabetic mothers, LGA/SGA, preterm: treat <47 mg/dL per local protocol (dextrose gel, feeding).

## Umbilicus and skin
Dry cord care; separation by ~2 weeks (delayed >1 month → consider leukocyte adhesion defect). Omphalitis (spreading redness, discharge, fever) → emergency antibiotics. Umbilical granuloma: silver nitrate. Diaper candidiasis with satellite lesions → topical antifungal.

## Prevention
- Vitamin K at birth (refusal raises risk of late vitamin K deficiency bleeding — bleeding, bruising, or intracranial hemorrhage at 2–12 weeks).
- Vitamin D 400 IU daily for breastfed and partially breastfed infants; iron supplementation from ~4 months for exclusively breastfed term infants until iron-rich foods.
- Newborn metabolic screen, hearing screen, CCHD pulse-oximetry screen.
- Safe sleep (AAP 2022): back to sleep, firm flat separate surface, room-sharing without bed-sharing for at least 6 months, no soft bedding, inclined sleepers, or weighted sleep products; avoid smoke exposure; offer a pacifier at sleep.
- Rear-facing car seat. Screen the parent for postpartum depression at 1-, 2-, 4- and 6-month visits.
